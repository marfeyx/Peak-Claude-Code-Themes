# kyoto

> A calm Kyoto valley — sakura, a waterfall and a slow river

Livio's `kyoto` renderer, bridged onto the Base Theme-switcher. A spring valley
drawn above the readout: layered hills, five sakura in blossom, a vermillion
torii at the water's edge, a river across the foreground and petals drifting
down over all of it.

It is deliberately the quiet one. The light never changes, nothing flashes,
nothing escalates, and the context meter is a branch coming into blossom rather
than an alarm. Livio wrote it as a companion to `vice` — that one is a clock and
a siren, this one is a window.

## What's in here

| File | Goes to | Whose | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kyoto.sh` | `~/.claude/statusline/themes/` | ours | the theme the framework loads |
| `kyoto.py` | `vendor/livio/` | **Livio's, verbatim** | the valley |
| `kyotogif.py` | `vendor/livio/` | **Livio's, verbatim** | **not optional** — `kyoto.py` does `import kyotogif as art` and pulls twenty art constants, plus `noise`, `shade` and `mix`, out of it. Its `build_all` generator is never reached. |
| `preview.ansi` | — | — | a real render in colour, 100 columns. `cat preview.ansi` |
| `install.sh` | — | — | copies everything into place and selects it |

Plus `../shared/`. `kyotogif.py` is the easy trap in this bundle: it looks like
a pure image generator from its name, and dropping it means the theme does not
render at all.

## What it looks like

Fourteen rows at a full-width terminal: twelve scene rows and two HUD rows. The
scene is drawn on a 2×4 octant pixel grid, so twelve terminal rows carry
forty-eight rows of picture — which is why the hills have gradients and the
torii has an actual curve to its lintel.

Geometry is proportional to the panel, not absolute, so it holds at any height.
Reading down from the top, with the fractions the renderer uses:

1. **Sky,** filled from the top down to the waterline rather than only to the
   horizon — the ridges dip below the horizon in places, and anything the hills
   did not cover would otherwise be left transparent and read as black bands
   across the valley. Gradient from `SKY_TOP` to `SKY_LOW` on a 0.9 power curve.

2. **Three hill layers** at 1.20×, 1.55× and 1.95× the horizon, each a sum of
   three sine waves at different frequencies and seeds, each with a lighter
   ridge line along its top edge. Far hill, mid hill, forest.

3. **Five sakura** at 10 %, 30 %, 52 %, 76 % and 93 % of the width. Trunk
   heights and crown radii are hashed per tree so they are not clones; each
   sways on its own phase at 0.22 rad/s. The crown is an ellipse — half-width
   horizontally — shaded from dark at the bottom to light at the top with noise
   mixed in, because a flat pink blob does not read as blossom.

4. **The torii** at 80 % of the width, standing on the waterline: two uprights,
   a curved upper lintel whose lift is quadratic in the distance from centre,
   and a straight lower one.

5. **The river,** from 62 % to 84 % of the height. A gradient from `WATER_FAR`
   to `WATER_NEAR` with foam speckle where a noise field crosses 0.982, sampled
   at `x + t × 6`, so the texture drifts downstream at six pixels per second.

6. **The near bank,** from 84 % down, grass noise with occasional fallen petals.

7. **Drifting petals** over everything, one per twelve columns, falling from the
   crowns.

8. **HUD row one** — the wall clock, the path, the branch, the diffstat.

9. **HUD row two** — the blossom meter, cost, weekly spend, model, effort,
   duration.

**The blossom meter** is the context window as a branch coming into flower:
twelve cells, `❀` for used and `·` for free. Measured: 1 petal at 8 % context,
6 at 47 %, 11 at 92 %. The colour ramps green → amber → red at 50 % and 75 %,
which is the only concession to alarm anywhere in the theme.

**Animation** is limited to the river drift, the tree sway and the petals,
all derived from `SL_NOW`, which the bridge pins. Two renders of the same second
are byte-identical. The light is fixed by design — unlike `vice`, the clock does
not change the scene, only the HUD.

## What data it shows

| Row | Shown | Field |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | wall clock, `10:00` | `SL_NOW`, pinned by the bridge |
| 1 | current path | `SL_PATH`, shortened to 34 characters |
| 1 | git branch | resolved by the host, never forked per frame |
| 1 | session diffstat | `+696` / `-276` |
| 2 | context used as a blossom meter | `SL_CONTEXT_PERCENT` |
| 2 | session cost | `$222.07` |
| 2 | weekly gateway spend | spend / budget / share; absent in preview mode |
| 2 | model display name | `Opus 5 (1M context)` |
| 2 | reasoning effort | `xhigh effort`, and `fast` when fast mode is on |
| 2 | session duration | `5 hours 51 minutes` |

Not read: token counts, ahead/behind counts, remaining-context percentage,
output style, version, session id, remote repo identity, API duration, and the
thinking / ultracode / exceeds-200k flags.

## How it behaves as the terminal narrows

Measured by rendering the bundled sample payload at **every width from 20 to
180 columns**. The framework subtracts a 4-column safety margin, so the theme
budgets against `SL_COLUMNS = COLUMNS − 4`.

**Height** is `min(requested, SL_COLUMNS ÷ 3)`, floored at 5 scene rows. The
default request is 12:

| Terminal columns | Total rows |
|---|---|
| 20 – 21 | 7 |
| 22 – 24 | 8 |
| 25 – 27 | 9 |
| 28 – 30 | 10 |
| 31 – 33 | 11 |
| 34 – 36 | 12 |
| 37 – 39 | 13 |
| ≥ 40 | 14 — full height, and it stops growing |

**The two HUD rows** shed whole segments at separator boundaries. Row 2 fills
before row 1 gets anything beyond the clock, which is a little surprising the
first time:

| Terminal columns | HUD |
|---|---|
| 20 – 21 | `10:00` / `ctx ❀❀❀❀❀❀······` — the percentage itself does not fit |
| 22 – 24 | the meter gains ` 47%`, a character at a time |
| 25 – 37 | both rows stable, nothing more fits |
| 38 – 39 | row 2 gains `$222.07` |
| 40 – 63 | row 1 gains the path |
| 64 – 82 | row 2 gains `Opus 5 (1M context)` |
| 83 – 107 | row 2 gains `xhigh effort` |
| 108 – 116 | row 2 gains `5 hours 51 minutes` |
| 117 – 132 | row 1 gains the git branch |
| ≥ 133 | row 1 gains the diffstat `+696/-276` |

The branch here is **70 characters** long, which is why it costs until 117
columns. That number is payload-dependent; a short branch appears far earlier.
There is no truncation step between "whole branch" and "no branch" — the bridge
sheds at separators, so a field is either fully present or absent.

**Under `NO_COLOR`** the theme collapses to three rows: a single `─` rule
standing in for the valley, plus the two HUD rows with the meter rendered as
`ctx ******...... 47%`. No escapes are emitted at all.

## Requirements

- **`python3`**, stdlib only.
- **A font carrying Unicode 16 octants** — Symbols for Legacy Computing
  Supplement, U+1CD00–U+1CDE5, plus `🮂 🮅` from the Legacy Computing block.
  **Treat this as mandatory.** CaskaydiaMono NF has them; Cascadia Mono and
  Ubuntu Mono do not, and the valley comes out as a field of tofu boxes.
  Livio documents `SL_KYOTO_PIXELS=half` as the fallback for exactly this
  case — **it does not work.** See Known issues.
- **A truecolour terminal.** Under `NO_COLOR` you get the three-row text
  fallback, which is legible but is not a valley.
- **bash 4.2+** for the framework.
- **Cost: about 284 ms per render** at 100 columns on this machine, measured
  over five warm renders, including Python startup. It is the most expensive of
  the four — the octant packer runs an exhaustive best-pair colour search per
  cell. The refresh is about once a second, so it fits, with less headroom than
  the others.
- Platform-neutral. The Windows Terminal background version of the valley is
  deliberately not shipped (see [`../README.md`](../README.md)).

## Known issues

**`SL_KYOTO_PIXELS=half` is broken.** Setting it produces twelve identical rows
of `▀` and no scenery at all. This is **upstream behaviour, not the bridge** —
verified by importing `kyoto.py` directly, outside this framework, with the same
result. `make_layer` is handed pixel dimensions sized for the 2×4 octant grid
and `Pixels` interprets them as 1×2, so every drawn pixel lands off-canvas and
only the sky fill survives.

Nothing here works around it, because nothing here edits Livio's source — that
is the whole point of the vendoring arrangement, and a one-line patch would have
to be re-applied on every refresh from him. The practical consequence is simply
that this theme needs an octant-capable font. The fix belongs upstream, in
`kyoto.py`'s `make_layer`.

## Install

With the Base Theme-switcher already at `~/.claude/statusline/`:

```sh
./install.sh
```

By hand:

```sh
mkdir -p ~/.claude/statusline/vendor/livio
cp ../shared/*.py ../shared/wrapper.sh ~/.claude/statusline/vendor/livio/
cp kyoto.py kyotogif.py ~/.claude/statusline/vendor/livio/
cp kyoto.sh ~/.claude/statusline/themes/
```

Then `/sl kyoto`, or `/sl 41` from the `@order: 41` header.

Check it:

```sh
~/.claude/statusline/check.sh kyoto
```

Renders at 30/40/50/60/80/100/160/220 columns plus `NO_COLOR`. **The copy in
this folder passes with zero failures.** Nothing else is copied anywhere: no
font, no wallpaper, no shader, no terminal configuration.

## Tuning

| Variable | Default | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `SL_KYOTO_ROWS` | `12` | panel height, clamped to 5…24 and again by the width |
| `SL_KYOTO_PIXELS` | octant | `half` is documented upstream and does not work — see Known issues |

## Preview

```sh
cat preview.ansi
```

and against your own live session at any width:

```sh
SL_PREVIEW=1 COLUMNS=60 ~/.claude/statusline/statusline.sh --theme kyoto \
  < ~/.claude/statusline/sample-payload.json
```

`SL_FAKE_NOW=<epoch>` pins the clock if you want a reproducible frame — that is
how `preview.ansi` was made.

## Uninstall

```sh
rm ~/.claude/statusline/themes/kyoto.sh
```

Then `/sl <something-else>`. The modules under `vendor/livio/` are shared with
the other three themes; only remove `kyoto*.py` if none of those are installed.
